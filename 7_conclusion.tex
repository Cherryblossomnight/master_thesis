\section{Conclusion of this thesis}
In this thesis, a hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was proposed for contact-rich surface sliding tasks using multi-link aerial robots. In such tasks, the coupling between unmodeled surface geometry and frictional effects requires the aerial robot to exhibit both robustness and compliant behavior simultaneously. We attempted to combine the advantages of impedance and admittance control by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robots.

First, impedance control was introduced to regulate the CoG motion of multi-link aerial robots. Under impedance control, the multi-link aerial robots can exhibit different responses to external forces by adjusting the mass ratio $\mu$. By selecting an appropriate mass ratio, a more rigid response can be achieved in the sliding directions. This characteristic helps the aerial robot resist friction-induced disturbances during sliding.

Second, admittance control was introduced to utilize the deformation capability of the multi-link structure. Under admittance control, the multi-link aerial robots can deform in response to external forces, forming a response cycle between geometric variations, external forces, and structural deformation. This response cycle enables the multi-link aerial robots to adapt to geometric variations introduced by uneven and unmodeled surfaces.

Based on the above characteristics, the hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was developed by applying impedance and admittance control in different task directions. Impedance control was applied in the sliding directions, while admittance control was applied in the contact direction. By combining their complementary characteristics, the proposed framework enables the multi-link aerial robots to simultaneously resist disturbances in the sliding directions and adapt to geometric variations in the contact direction.

In the experimental part, the effects of the proposed hybrid control framework were demonstrated. The practical parameter adjustment process was discussed, and the responses of impedance and admittance control to external forces were demonstrated through preliminary experiments. The underactuated multi-link aerial robot Hydrus Xi was used to evaluate the hybrid controller through comparisons among four controllers. The results showed that stable surface sliding could be achieved under the hybrid controller, while the effects of impedance and admittance control were both maintained in their corresponding directions. Therefore, the hybrid controller could combine the capabilities of both control methods in the surface sliding task. An extension experiment on the overactuated multi-link aerial robot DRAGON showed that the proposed framework could also be applied to another type of multi-link aerial robot.

Overall, this thesis demonstrated that the different characteristics of impedance and admittance control can be combined by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robots. The proposed framework enables multi-link aerial robots to achieve resilient sliding and adaptive contact simultaneously, providing a new control approach for contact-rich surface sliding tasks with uncertain surface conditions.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

Although the capability of the proposed framework is demonstrated, there are still several aspects that can be further improved.

First, the controller parameters were mainly tuned based on experimental experience in this thesis, which cannot guarantee optimal performance. In addition, the constant parameters may not adapt to changing configurations and more complex environments. In future work, we will improve the parameter tuning process by introducing learning-based methods.

Second, the admittance behavior demonstrated in this thesis was mainly restricted to a single direction. However, if the configuration constraints introduced in this thesis are relaxed, geometric variations can be considered in 3D space, and other types of aerial manipulation tasks may also require 3D motion. Therefore, the admittance control can be extended to 3D and implemented on DRAGON, which is capable of 3D deformation. In future work, 3D admittance control can be conducted for more complex aerial manipulation tasks, such as interaction with complex curved surfaces with larger geometric variations or grasping an irregularly shaped object using the two ends of DRAGON.

Third, the current hybrid control framework mainly focused on the behavior during contact, while the contact establishment process has not been investigated. In our experiments, we observed so-called jumping behavior during initial contact, which could lead to instability and even crashes. Based on our experimental observations and simulations, this behavior may be related to the mass ratio $\mu$ along the contact direction. Future work will further investigate the contact establishment process and its relationship with the dynamic parameters, and develop suitable control strategies for smoother contact.

In conclusion, future work will focus on improving the adaptability of the proposed framework to more complex configurations, contact processes, and interaction environments.
