\section{Conclusion of this thesis}
In this thesis, a hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was proposed for contact-rich surface sliding tasks using multi-link aerial robots. In such tasks, the coupling between unmodeled surface geometry and frictional effects requires the aerial robot to exhibit both robustness and compliant behavior simultaneously. We attempted to combine the advantages of impedance and admittance control by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robots.

First, impedance control was introduced to regulate the CoG motion of multi-link aerial robots. Under impedance control, the multi-link aerial robots can exhibit different responses to external forces by adjusting the mass ratio $\mu$, which helps to provide higher resistance to friction-induced disturbances during sliding.

Second, admittance control was introduced to utilize the deformation capability of the multi-link structure. Under admittance control, the multi-link aerial robots can deform in response to external forces, which can be used to adapt to geometric variations introduced by uneven and unmodeled surfaces.

Based on the above characteristics, the hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was developed by applying impedance and admittance control in different task directions. In the sliding directions affected by friction, impedance control helps to follow the desired trajectory against external disturbances. In the contact direction where geometric variations occur, admittance control helps to adapt to these variations through structural deformation. Combining these two control methods enables the multi-link aerial robots to deal with the different requirements of surface sliding tasks.


In the experimental part, the effects of the proposed hybrid control framework were demonstrated. The parameter adjustment experience was discussed, and the effects of impedance and admittance control in response to external forces were demonstrated through preliminary experiments. The underactuated multi-link aerial robot Hydrus Xi was used to demonstrate the hybrid control effect through comparisons among four controllers. The results showed that the hybrid controller could combine the advantages of impedance and admittance control. An extension experiment on the overactuated multi-link aerial robot DRAGON demonstrated that the proposed framework could also be applied to another type of multi-link aerial robot.

Overall, this thesis demonstrated that the different characteristics of impedance and admittance control can be combined by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robots. The proposed framework enables the multi-link aerial robots to achieve resilient sliding and adaptive contact simultaneously, providing a novel control approach for aerial interaction tasks involving uncertain contact situations.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}

Although the capability of the proposed framework is demonstrated, there are still several aspects that can be further improved.

First, the controller parameters were mainly tuned based on experimental experience in this thesis. The constant parameters may not adapt to changing configurations and more complex environments. In future work, we will improve the parameter tuning process by introducing learning-based methods.

Second, the admittance behavior demonstrated in this thesis was mainly restricted to one direction. However, admittance control can actually be achieved in three dimensions by DRAGON. In future work, 3D admittance control can be conducted for more complex aerial manipulation tasks, such as interaction with complex curved surfaces with larger geometric variations.

Third, the current hybrid control framework mainly focuses on the behavior during contact, while the contact establishment process has not been investigated. In our experiments, we observed so-called jumping behavior during initial contact. Future work will further investigate this contact process and develop suitable control strategies for smoother contact.

In conclusion, future work will focus on improving the adaptability of the proposed framework to more complex configurations, contact processes, and interaction environments.
