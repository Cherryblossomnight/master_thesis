\section{Conclusion of this thesis}
In this thesis, a hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was proposed for contact-rich surface sliding tasks using multi-link aerial robots. In such tasks, a coupling between unmodeled surface geometry and frictional effects requires the aerial robot to exhibit both robustness and compliant behavior simultaneously. We attempted to combine the adventages of impedance and admittance control by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robots.

First, impedance control was introduced to regulate the CoG motion of the multi-link aerial robots. Under the impedance control, the multi-link aerial robots can exhibit different response to the external forces by redesigning the mass ratio $\mu$, which helps to provide higher resistance to the friction-induced disturbances during sliding.

Second, admittance control was introduced to utilize the deformation capability of the multi-link structure. Under the admittance control, the multi-link aerial robots can deform in respose to the exteral forces, which can be used to adapt to the geometric variations intruduced by the uneven and unmodeled surfaces.

Based on the above characteristics, the hybrid impedance--admittance control framework was developed by applying impedance and admittance control in different task directions. In the sliding directions affected by friction, the impedance control helps to follow the desired trajectory against external disturbances. In the contact direction where geometric variation occur, the admittance control helps to adapt these variations through structural deformation. Combining these two control methods together enables the multi-link aerial robots to deal with the different requirements of the surface sliding tasks.

In the experiments part, the effects of the proposed hybrid control framework were demonstrated. The parameter adjustment experience was discussed, and the effects of impedance and admitance control in response to the external force demonstrated through preliminary experiments. The 2D multi-link aerial robot Hydrus Xi was used to demonstrated the hybrid control effect through comparisons among four controller. The results showed that the hybrid controller could combine the advantages of impedance and admittance control. An extension experiment on the 3D multi-link aerial robot DRAGON demonstrated that the proposed framework could also be applied to another type of multi-link aerial robot.

Overall, this thesis demonstrated that the different characteristics of impedance and admittance control can be combined by utilizing the deformable structure of multi-link aerial robot. The proposed framework enables multi-link aerial robots to achieve resilient sliding and adaptive contact simultaneously, providing a novel control approach for aerial interaction tasks involving uncertain contact situation.

\section{Future work}

Although the capability of proposed framework is demonstrated, there are still several aspects that can be further improved.

First, the controller parameters were mainly tuned based on experimental experience in this thesis. The constant parameters may not adapt to the changing configuration and more complex enviroments. In future work, We will improve the parameter tuning process by introduing learning-based methods.

Second, the admittance behavior demonstrated in this thesis was mainly restricted to one direction. However, the admittance control actually can be achieved in 3D directions by DARGON. In future work, 3D admittance control can be conducted for more complex aerial manipulation tasks, such as complex curved surfaces with larger geometric variations.

Thrid, the current hybrid control framework mainly focuses on the behavior during contact, while the contact establishment process has not been investigated. In author's experience, we observed so-called jumping behavior during initial contact. Future work will further investigate this contact process and develop suitable control strategies for smoother contact.

In conclusion, future work will focus on improving the adaptability of the proposed framework to more complex configurations, contact processes, and interaction environments.
