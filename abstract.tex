\section{Introduction}
Aerial manipulation has attracted significant attention due to its potential for performing complex tasks. In surface sliding tasks, an aerial robot must maintain contact with an unknown uneven surface while following a desired trajectory \cite{bodie_2020_active}. Uncertainties in surface geometry and friction introduce variations in interaction forces, requiring both robustness against force disturbances and adaptability to changing contact conditions. Nevertheless, existing aerial robot controllers remain limited in achieving this combination. Solutions are needed in both mechanical structures and control methods.

For the mechanical structures, articulated multi-link aerial robots can actively deform in flight, providing additional degrees of freedom for aerial manipulation \cite{zhao_2021_singularity}. For the control methods, impedance and admittance control have been commonly used for aerial interaction. However, impedance control provides robustness against interaction-force disturbances, while admittance control provides compliance to geometric variations. Their complementary yet opposite causal relationships make them difficult to combine on the same platform.

In this work, we propose a hybrid impedance--admittance control strategy that exploits two actuation sources of a multi-link aerial robot. Rotor thrust governs the global motion and realizes impedance control for disturbance-resilient interaction, while joint actuation regulates the end-effector pose and generates admittance behavior to adapt to unknown surface geometry. This coordinated control enables robust and adaptive sliding on unknown surfaces, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:main_figure}.

\section{Control}
Impedance control models the robot as a second-order system in the sight of environment, allowing the robot can adjusting its own movement by external force. Its dynamic parameters can be designed to achieve different interaction behaviors \cite{bodie_2020_active}. In this work, the translational and rotational command wrenches of impedance control are given by

\vspace{-5mm}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:imp cmd for trans2}
    {^W\!\boldsymbol{f}_{cmd}}=(m\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}_d-\boldsymbol{E}_3){^W\!\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}_{ext}}-m(\tilde{\boldsymbol{C}}_{td}{\boldsymbol{e}_v}+{\tilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{td}}{\boldsymbol{e}_p})+m\boldsymbol{g}.
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:imp cmd for rot2}
    {^{C}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{cmd}}=(\boldsymbol{I}\boldsymbol{I}^{-1}_d-\boldsymbol{E}_3){^{C}}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\tau}}_{ext}-\boldsymbol{I}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{C}}_{rd}{\boldsymbol{e}_\omega}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{rd}{\boldsymbol{e}_\alpha})
    +{^{C}\boldsymbol{\omega}} \times \boldsymbol{I}{^{C}\boldsymbol{\omega}}.
\end{equation}

Admittance control produces compliant displacement in response to external forces. In this work, it regulates the end-effector position error by adjusting the joint angles in response to forces induced by surface geometric variations, thereby mitigating force and position disturbances experienced by the CoG through deformation. The admittance dynamics along the contact direction can be written as
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admittance control}
    m_a(\ddot{r}-\ddot{r}_{ref})+c_a(\dot{r}-\dot{r}_{ref})+k_a(r-r_{ref})=\hat{f}_{ext,x}-f_{ref,x}.
\end{equation}

In surface-sliding tasks, the sliding directions are mainly affected by friction disturbances, whereas the contact direction is affected by geometric variations. Therefore, the hybrid impedance--admittance controller assigns suitable control behavior to each axis. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid_controller}, admittance control is applied along the contact direction ($x$ axis) to adapt to geometric variations, while impedance control governs the sliding directions ($y$ and $z$ axes) against friction disturbances. Thus, the two controllers provide geometric adaptability and disturbance robustness through different actuation sources.

\section{Experiment}
In the hybrid control experiment, Hydrus Xi was used as the primary experimental platform. The reference trajectory was defined on a plane perpendicular to the $x$ axis, where the robot followed a circular trajectory with a radius of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ and a period of $14\,\mathrm{s}$. Due to the unknown inclination of the board, approximately $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ of geometric variation occurred along the $x$ axis during one cycle, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:experiment_scenario}(a). This variation was not incorporated into the reference trajectory or controller design, requiring online adaptation during contact.

To evaluate the proposed framework, four controllers were compared: baseline PD, impedance, admittance, and hybrid impedance--admittance controllers. In the hybrid controller, impedance control was applied along the $y$ and $z$ axes against sliding disturbances, while admittance control was applied along the $x$ axis to adapt to geometric variations. The impedance-only controller employed no admittance-based joint adaptation, whereas the admittance-only controller regulated the CoG using baseline PD control. The experimental process is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:experiment_scenario}(b) and (c).

Table~\ref{tb:hybrid} compares the four controllers. The impedance-only controller achieved good tracking performance along the $y$ and $z$ axes, whereas the admittance-only controller resulted in relatively small CoG position variation along the $x$ axis. The hybrid controller maintained competitive performance across all four metrics, combining the advantages of both controllers in the sliding and contact directions.

The proposed framework was further applied to the 3D multi-link aerial robot DRAGON. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:experiment_scenario}(d) and (e), DRAGON deforms its multi-link structure during surface contact to adapt to geometric variations, further demonstrating the applicability of the framework to different multi-link aerial robots.

\section{Conclusion}
In this thesis, we presented a hybrid impedance--admittance control framework for multi-link aerial robots performing surface-sliding tasks. Impedance control provides resilience against friction-induced disturbances, while admittance control utilizes structural deformation to adapt to unknown surface geometry. By assigning the two controllers to different task directions, the proposed method exploits the interaction characteristics of each axis. Experiments with Hydrus Xi demonstrated the advantages of the hybrid controller over individual methods, while the DRAGON experiment further showed its applicability to different multi-link aerial robots.
