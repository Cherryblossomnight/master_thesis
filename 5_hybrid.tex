The motivation for this hybrid impedance--admittance control framework is to provide both resilience and adaptivity when sliding on an unmodeled surface, where geometric variations and friction introduce significant uncertainties. In this framework, impedance control is used to improve the response to friction-induced disturbances in the sliding directions, while admittance control is used to adapt the configuration of the multi-link aerial robots in response to geometric variations in the contact direction. Therefore, these two control methods can provide different behaviors on the same multi-link aerial robot platform. Although impedance and admittance control have opposite causalities, they are realized through different actuation sources of the multi-link aerial robots in the proposed framework. This separation allows the two control methods to be implemented simultaneously for their respective interaction objectives.

% The combination of impedance and admittance control is realized through different actuation sources of the multi-link aerial robot. The impedance controller mainly determines the desired wrench of the overall aerial robot, which is realized through the rotor thrusts. In contrast, the admittance controller generates the desired deformation according to the external force, which is realized through joint motion. Therefore, the two controllers can be simultaneously implemented on the same multi-link aerial robot without directly acting on the same control variable.

From the implementation perspective, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, impedance control manages the overall state of the aerial robot, which is represented by the pose of its CoG, while admittance control regulates the end effector states through the adjustment of the joint angles. In the control formulation, the two controllers use different actuation sources and can therefore be considered separately. However, their execution order needs to be considered in the actual implementation. The joint motion generated by the admittance controller changes the configuration of the multi-link aerial robot, which further affects the thrust allocation. Therefore, the joint configuration is first updated through the admittance controller and inverse kinematics, and the updated configuration is then taken into account in the thrust computation.

\section{Hybrid Control Mechanism for Multi-Link Aerial Robots}

In surface-sliding tasks, the contact and sliding directions exhibit fundamentally different physical characteristics: the sliding directions are dominated by friction-induced disturbances, whereas the contact direction corresponds to the direction of the equivalently represented geometric variations of the environment. Leveraging this distinction, the hybrid impedance--admittance controller assigns each axis the control behavior best suited to its interaction properties.  As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid_controller}, admittance control is applied along the contact direction ($x$ axis) to regulate geometry-driven variations, while impedance control governs the sliding directions ($y$ and $z$ axes) to regulate the response to friction-induced disturbances. Both control methods define second-order dynamic relationships to reshape the response of the multi-link aerial robot to the environment.

This directional assignment is based on the characteristics of impedance and admittance control discussed in Chapters \ref{ch:impedance} and \ref{ch:admittance}. As discussed in Chapter \ref{ch:impedance}, impedance control can reshape the dynamic behavior of multi-link aerial robots through parameter design. In particular, the mass ratio $\mu$ is related to the desired stiffness of the system. Since resistance to friction-induced disturbances is required in the sliding directions, impedance control is used in these directions to regulate the CoG motion. By selecting suitable parameters, the multi-link aerial robots can exhibit a more rigid response to external disturbances while maintaining safe interaction, thereby improving their resistance to friction-induced disturbances.

On the other hand, admittance control can provide adaptation to geometric variations through the response cycle introduced in Chapter \ref{ch:admittance}. In this cycle, geometric variations generate changes in the external force, which further induce deformation of the multi-link structure. The resulting deformation compensates for the geometric variations and can therefore be regarded as adaptation to changes in the surface geometry. Therefore, admittance control is applied along the contact direction to provide adaptive behavior in response to geometric variations.

Overall, the combination of impedance and admittance control fully utilizes their own advantages, therefore achieve the requirements of the surface sliding tasks. We need to emphasize that neither impedance nor admittance control alone can satisfy both requirements of surface sliding tasks. Therefore, both control characteristics are required to simultaneously address the different interaction requirements in the contact and sliding directions.

Through this task-aligned division of control responsibilities, the hybrid controller is designed to provide resilient behavior in the sliding directions and adaptive behavior in the contact direction. This directional decomposition is enabled by the actuation structure of multi-link aerial robots, which allows the two control paradigms to be realized simultaneously through independent actuation sources. The proposed combination therefore provides a control framework for addressing both interaction requirements of surface-sliding tasks. The performance of the proposed hybrid impedance--admittance controller will be further demonstrated in the experiment chapter.

\section{Summary}
In this chapter, we discussed how to integrate impedance and admittance control on the same multi-link aerial robot platform. We also introduced the hybrid control mechanism and explained why this framework can be used in surface sliding tasks. Based on the different interaction characteristics of the contact and sliding directions, impedance and admittance control are assigned to different directions according to their respective characteristics. Through this directional assignment, the proposed framework is designed to provide resilient behavior in the sliding directions and adaptive behavior in the contact direction. The performance of the proposed hybrid impedance--admittance controller will be demonstrated in the experiment chapter.
